## Naming

- **Marks.** Every ISC2 page footer: "ISC2, CISSP, SSCP, CCSP, CGRC, CSSLP, HCISPP, ISSAP, ISSEP, ISSMP,
  CC, and CBK are registered marks of ISC2, Inc." The 2023 press release wrote "(CGRC)™" and the PDF
  outline shows "TM"; the current footer and member policies treat CGRC as a registered mark.
- **Copyright.** Footer: "All contents of this site constitute the property of ISC2, Inc. and may not be
  copied, reproduced or distributed without prior written permission."
- **Marks rules (member policies).** Rules "apply to all ISC2 certification marks … ISSAP, ISSEP, ISSMP,
  CISSP, CCSP, CSSLP, HCISPP, CGRC, SSCP, and CC". A mark may not imply "ISC2's affiliation, sponsorship,
  endorsement, certification, or approval"; marks carry ® except where size prevents; marks are never
  translated. Never pluralise "CGRC".
- **NDA.** Candidates agree "That you may not disclose the Exam questions, items or answers or discuss
  any of the content of the Exam Materials with any person without prior written approval of ISC2".
- **How CONTENT-POLICY §4 applies.** ISC2 is not a §5 body. Name the exam in plain text, to state a true
  fact, with our brand first. Write "CGRC®", use no logo, and never say "official", "approved" or
  "certified". Title: "[Site name] deck for the ISC2 Certified in Governance, Risk and Compliance
  (CGRC®) exam". "Formerly CAP" is a true fact and may appear once in the description, citing the
  press release. The outline is not openly licensed: link to it, never copy it (§2.3). The CBK, "Official
  ISC2 Training", the "Ultimate Guide", the "CGRC Flash Cards", "Knowledge Check" and the framework guides
  linked from the CGRC page are never sources (§2.2). The references page's books are not sources either;
  only its NIST items are used, from NIST.
- **Deck notice text:**

> ISC2, CGRC, CC and CBK are registered marks of ISC2, Inc. This deck is independent
> and is not affiliated with, sponsored, endorsed or approved by ISC2, Inc. It
> contains no ISC2 exam content and is not built from ISC2 study materials.

- **Attribution.** NIST works: cite each publication, then "Republished courtesy of the National
  Institute of Standards and Technology." (NIST licensing page, as for isc2-sscp). SP 800-18r2 and SP
  800-60 say they are "not subject to copyright" and "Attribution would … be appreciated by NIST". US
  Code, CFR and FedRAMP pages: cite the section or page. GDPR and EU AI Act: cite the article. FedRAMP:
  "The FedRAMP name and the FedRAMP logo are the property of the General Services Administration (GSA)",
  so use the name in plain text only.

## Traps

- **CAP is the old name, and old CAP material uses old domains.** The 2023 rename notice lists the
  domains then as "Information Security Risk Management Program", "Scope of the Information System", …,
  "Authorization/Approval of Information Systems", "Continuous Monitoring". The current outline (June
  2024) calls them "System Compliance" and "Compliance Maintenance" and adds frameworks beyond the RMF. [T1]
- **Not adaptive.** 125 fixed items in 3 hours; CGRC is not in ISC2's CAT list. Do not carry over CISSP or
  SSCP CAT rules (minimum items, no review). [T1]
- **Associate window is 3 years** (CISSP and CCSP six, SSCP two). Price is US$599, not SSCP's US$249. [T1]
- **SP 800-18 Rev. 1 is withdrawn.** CSRC: "Withdrawn on June 30, 2026 … Superseded by SP 800-18 Rev. 2".
  Rev. 2 (June 2026) is retitled "Developing Security, Privacy, and Cybersecurity Supply Chain Risk
  Management Plans for Systems" and treats the security plan, privacy plan and C-SCRM plan as "system
  plans". ISC2's references page still lists "NIST SP 800-18, Rev. 1". Card system plans from Rev. 2. [T7]
- **SP 800-60 Rev. 1 is still current, but a Rev. 2 is coming.** CSRC shows Vol. 1 Rev. 1 and Vol. 2
  Rev. 1 (August 2008) as final, and "SP 800-60 Rev. 2 (Initial Working Draft)" of 31 January 2024. The
  draft says NIST intends to fold SP 800-122 guidance into SP 800-60 "and withdraw SP 800-122". [T5]
- **SP 800-53 Release 5.2.0.** On 27 August 2025 NIST issued minor releases 5.2.0 of SP 800-53, 800-53A
  and 800-53B: new SA-15(13), SA-24 and SI-02(07), in response to "Executive Order 14306"; 800-53B has
  "no changes to the baselines". The cached PDF shows "INCLUDES UPDATES AS OF 12-10-2020"; the release
  lives in the CPRT catalogue. Rev. 5 is still the revision; no Rev. 6 draft is listed on CSRC. [T6]
- **No newer drafts for the core.** CSRC's draft-SP search lists no draft of SP 800-37, 800-53, 800-30,
  800-39, 800-137 or 800-18; the only draft among the core documents is SP 800-60 Rev. 2. SP 800-37 Rev. 2
  (December 2018), 800-30 Rev. 1 (September 2012), 800-39 (March 2011), 800-137 (September 2011), FIPS 199
  (February 2004), FIPS 200 (March 2006) and CSF 2.0 (26 February 2024) are the current finals. [T1] [T2]
- **Seven RMF steps, not six.** SP 800-37r2: "a preparatory step … and six main steps". After Prepare the
  steps "can be carried out in a nonsequential order". "The addition of the Prepare step is one of the key changes to the RMF", so CAP-era material built on Rev. 1 lacks it. [T2]
- **Four authorisation decisions.** "Authorization to operate; Common control authorization;
  Authorization to use; and Denial of authorization". "An ATU requires a completed ATO by the organization
  providing the shared or cloud system". The phrase "interim authorization" does not occur in Rev. 2: a short ATO
  with operating restrictions covers live testing. Rescission is "a special case of authorization denial". [T10]
- **Initial vs ongoing vs reauthorisation.** Initial is a "zero-based review"; ongoing is "subsequent
  (follow-on) risk determinations … at agreed-upon and documented frequencies" and depends "on a robust
  continuous monitoring program"; reauthorisation is "static, single point-in-time" and, under ongoing
  authorisation, usually event-driven. [T10]
- **Who does what.** The system owner or common control provider compiles and submits the package; only
  the AO accepts risk; the AO role "is an inherent U.S. Government function"; "The only activity that cannot be
  delegated by the authorizing official to the designated representative is the authorization decision and
  signing of the associated authorization decision document". [T2] [T10]
- **Five risk responses.** SP 800-39 lists "risk acceptance; risk avoidance; risk mitigation; risk sharing;
  risk transfer; or … a combination". The outline's 5.4 list matches. CC taught four; sharing is new. [T1] [T9]
- **"Not applicable" impact.** FIPS 199: "The potential impact value of not applicable only applies to the
  security objective of confidentiality." System impact uses the high water mark (FIPS 200). [T5]
- **FIPS are mandatory, SPs are guidance.** FIPS 200: "No provision is provided under FISMA for waivers to
  FIPS made mandatory by the Secretary of Commerce"; it sets minimum requirements "in seventeen
  security-related areas". SP 800-53 Rev. 5 has "20 families". Do not confuse 17 with 20. [T4] [T6]
- **Privacy baseline.** SP 800-53B has three security baselines plus "a privacy baseline that is applied to
  systems irrespective of impact level". [T6]
- **Findings wording.** SP 800-53A findings are "satisfied (S)" or "other than satisfied (O)"; the outline's
  "compliant, non-compliant, not applicable" is its own wording. [T9]
- **Assessment methods order.** The outline says "interview, examine, test"; SP 800-53A says "examine,
  interview, and test". Same three methods. [T8]
- **Control classes.** The outline's "management, technical, common, operational" mixes two schemes:
  common/hybrid/system-specific is the RMF's allocation scheme (SP 800-37r2); management/operational/
  technical classes were not found as a classification in SP 800-53 Rev. 5 (see Not verified). [T7]
- **CMMC numbers.** 32 CFR 170.4: "the 15 Level 1 requirements … the 110 Level 2 requirements from NIST SP
  800-171 R2 … and the 24 Level 3 requirements selected from NIST SP 800-172". Level 2 certification
  assessments are done by a C3PAO. [T4]
- **FISMA testing.** 44 U.S.C. 3554: testing "with a frequency depending on risk, but no less than
  annually". The outline expands FISMA as the "Federal Information Security Modernization Act"; the US Code
  source notes cite Pub. L. 113–283. SP 800-60 (2008) still says "Federal Information Security Management
  Act (FISMA) of 2002". [T4]
- **FedRAMP wording is moving.** The FedRAMP home page now says "FedRAMP Certified Cloud Solutions",
  counts "FedRAMP 20x Certified Services" and links "Consolidated Rules for 2026". Older material says
  "FedRAMP Authorized". Check the current rules before carding FedRAMP process. [T4]
- **Outline 1.2 on the web page is garbled.** It reads "Establishment of compliance program for the
  applicable framework 1.3 - Understand regulatory and legal requirements" before the real 1.3; the PDF
  has no such line. [T4]
- **AI items are only on the web page.** Tag them to T13, never to numbered objectives. [T13]
