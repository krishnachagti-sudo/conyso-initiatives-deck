## Traps

- `[T2]` **The outline is older than the rule.** Test topics still say "all air-conditioning and refrigeration equipment containing CFCs or HCFCs". Since 1 January 2018, 82.161 and 82.154 cover "any non-exempt substitute refrigerant" too: HFCs need certification, and the sales restriction applies to them.
- `[T7]` **Leak repair now covers ozone-depleting refrigerants only.** "Effective April 10, 2020, appliances with 50 or more pounds of substitute refrigerants are no longer subject to the requirements at 40 CFR 82.157" (regulatory updates page). 82.157(a): "the requirements of this section do not apply to appliances containing solely substitute refrigerants."
- `[T7]` **Old leak rates are still printed in the CFR.** 82.156(i) still holds the 35 percent (commercial and industrial process refrigeration) and 15 percent (comfort cooling) rates. They applied "only until January 1, 2019". The current rates are 20, 30 and 10 percent (82.157(c)(2)). Older study guides use the old numbers.
- `[T7]` **"More than 50" versus "50 or more".** The test-topics page says "containing more than 50 pounds". 82.157(a) and (c)(2) say "a full charge of 50 or more pounds". Cards follow the rule.
- `[T7]` **Follow-up verification test timing.** 82.157(e)(2): "within 10 days of the successful initial verification test". The EPA Q&A page says "within 30 days of the appliance's returning to normal operating characteristics". Old 82.156(i)(3) also says 30 days. Cards use the current 82.157 text and flag the Q&A wording.
- `[T2]` **There are four pressure classes, not two.** The outline says "high and low-pressure refrigerants". 82.152 defines very high (critical temperature below 104°F or above 355 psia at 104°F), high (170–355 psia), medium (45–170 psia) and low (below 45 psia). R-12 and R-134a are *medium*-pressure, yet Type II covers them: "medium-, high-, or very high-pressure appliances" (82.161(a)(1)(ii)). The EPA requirements page leaves out "medium".
- `[T6]` **80 percent has two triggers.** 80 percent applies with pre-15 November 1993 recovery equipment **or** when the compressor does not work. 90 percent needs both newer equipment **and** a working compressor. The alternative is 4 inches of mercury vacuum (82.156(b)).
- `[T7]` **The 1993 date line reads "on or after".** The CFR table says "manufactured or imported on or after November 15, 1993". The outline says "after".
- `[T7]` **Two 0 psig rules, opposite directions.** Before opening for a non-major repair, high-pressure appliances are *evacuated* to 0 psig, and low-pressure appliances are *pressurised* to 0 psig (82.156(a)(1)). Leaking components may be evacuated only "to the lowest level that can be attained", which "may not exceed 0 psig".
- `[T8]` **Low-pressure evacuation is in mm Hg absolute.** Low-pressure appliances go to "25 mm Hg absolute". All other levels are inches of Hg vacuum.
- `[T4]` **Recycle is not reclaim.** Recycled refrigerant goes back only to "equipment of the same owner". Refrigerant sold to a new owner must be reclaimed by an EPA-certified reclaimer to AHRI 700-2016 purity (82.152, 82.154(d)).
- `[T2]` **Disposal certification.** Disposing of small appliances, MVACs and MVAC-like appliances needs no certification (82.161(a)(1)(vii)). Disposing of other appliances does. Recovery equipment used before disposal must still meet the standards.
- `[T2]` **Section 609 is not Section 608.** MVAC technicians are certified under subpart B (Section 609). "Section 609 certified technicians cannot purchase refrigerants that are intended for use with stationary equipment, regardless of container size" (sales restriction page).
- `[T2]` **Small cans.** Uncertified buyers may still buy MVAC cans of "two pounds or less" of non-exempt substitute with a unique fitting and, if made or imported from 1 January 2018, a self-sealing valve (82.154(c)(1)(ix)). This is not a stationary-equipment exception.
- `[T2]` **The penalty figure moves every year.** 40 CFR 19.4 Table 1 gives $124,426 for 42 U.S.C. 7413(b), assessed on or after 8 January 2025. The previous column gives $121,275. Older figures still circulate: Table 2 lists $37,500 for earlier periods. The live test bank's figure is unknown. Cards should state the source and date.
- `[T2]` **R-22 is phased out, not banned.** "No production or import of HCFC-142b and HCFC-22" from 2020 (class II page). Yet "HCFC-22 (also called R-22) can be used to service existing equipment" (technician FAQ). All HCFCs end in 2030.
- `[T2]` **CFC date.** Class I (other than methyl bromide) production and import ended 1 January 1996. Halons ended earlier, on 1 January 1994 (class I page).
- `[T2]` **Venting exemptions are by end-use.** Propane, isobutane and R-441A are exempt only in listed end-uses. Propane in "retail food refrigeration—refrigerated food processing and dispensing equipment" applies only "effective July 15, 2024" (82.154(a)(1)(viii)).
- `[T2]` **"R-22a" is not R-22.** EPA: refrigerants named "22a" are "highly flammable substances that are not approved for use in existing air-conditioning systems" (technician resources page).
- `[T2]` **There is no "R-410A certification".** EPA: "EPA does not have a certification that is exclusively focused on R-410A" (Q&A).
- `[T2]` **Apprentice limits.** An apprentice must be registered with the Department of Labor Office of Apprenticeship, for at most two years, and "closely and continually supervised" (82.152, 82.161(a)(2)).
- `[T7]` **System-dependent limit.** System-dependent equipment may not be used on appliances with "more than 15 pounds", unless it is a permanently attached pump-out unit (82.156(e)).

## Languages

The exam is given by each programme. EPA's rule encourages "tests in other
languages or allowing the use of a translator when taking the test", and oral
tests on written request (82.161(b)(3)). No page fetched lists which
programmes offer which languages. EPA publishes a Spanish overview of the
programme (espanol.epa.gov, 3 September 2026) that repeats the types, the
25 + 25 structure and non-expiry. The test-topics page, the Q&A and the CFR
were fetched in English. I did not look for translations of them. The CFR is public domain. EPA's Spanish page falls
under the same EPA web policy as the English pages.
