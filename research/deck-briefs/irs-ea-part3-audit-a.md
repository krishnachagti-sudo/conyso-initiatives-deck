# irs-ea-part3 audit (part a): topics 1–3, 376 cards

Checked: 01-practice-duties (145), 02-sanctions-penalties (131), 03-authorisations (100): 376 cards. Every card was checked against its `evidence` first; claims beyond it were checked in research/sources/irs-ea-part3/ (31 CFR Part 10, 1 July 2025 edition, read column by column; Pub 947; Pub 216; Pub 594; Pub 908; Pub 1345; i2848; i8821; i8867 Rev. November 2025; IRM 4.10.7, 25.1.1, 25.1.2; the OPR, PTIN, TDS, enrolled-agent, delay-collection and Collection Financial Standards pages; 26 U.S.C. §§ 6060, 6103, 6107, 6109, 6662, 6663, 6694, 6695, 6702, 6713, 7216 with their amendment notes). No fetches were needed.

Covered opinions: only ea3.practice-duties.no-covered-opinions mentions them, and it teaches that they are gone. The 2025 edition's latest amending decision is T.D. 9668 (12 June 2014), as the card says.

Dollar figures: every figure drawn from a source now names its source and year and is marked volatile. Code amounts carry the section's last amendment year from the uscode notes (§ 6694: 2015; § 6695: 2018; § 6702: 2018; § 6662: 2025, subsection (m) only; §§ 7216 and 6713: 2019), so each is shown to be in force throughout 2025. Section 6695 amounts are statutory bases that § 6695(h) indexes, and the cards now say so. The only due diligence penalty sourced is $650 for returns filed in 2026 (i8867 p. 4); both cards that use it now warn that the exam uses 2025 amounts. Figures made up for a scenario are left as they are.

Scenarios: every date and sum was worked (1 March to 15 August = 167 days; 2 May to 20 June = 49 days; 18 months from 1 March 2026 = 1 September 2027; 5 years = 1 March 2031; return period ending 30 June 2026 + 3 years = 30 June 2029; 10% of $80,000 = $8,000 < $9,000; 180% and 250% of basis; 75% × $16,000 = $12,000; 50% × $3,000 = $1,500; 15% × $5,000 = $750; 2 × $650 = $1,300). All are correct. No scenario date is a filing deadline that falls on a weekend.
