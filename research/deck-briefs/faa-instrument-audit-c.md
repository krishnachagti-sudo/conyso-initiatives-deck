# faa-instrument audit C: topics 10–12

Scope: decks/faa-instrument/notes/10-arrivals-approach-procedures-and-minimums.json (107 cards), 11-landing-missed-approach-circling-and-visual-approaches.json (65) and 12-emergencies-and-abnormal-operations-under-ifr.json (41), 213 cards in all. None are pool cards. I checked every card against its `evidence` first. Where a claim went beyond the evidence, I grepped the cached text in research/sources/faa-instrument/: AIM 5-4 (procedure turns, circling, radar approaches, LP/LPV), 6-4 (radio failure), 7-1-13 (RVR) and 2-1-3 (REIL); IPH chapter 4 PDF pages 20 and 37; and IFH PDF pages 214, 330 and 332. I fetched nothing from the web.

I checked the rule cards with extra care: approach minimums, descent below DA/MDA and the visual reference list (91.175(b), (c), (d), (e), (h), (j)), missed approach, circling, and lost communications (91.185(b), (c)). They match the regulation text. The one gap is the procedure-turn card below.

Primers that re-teach private-pilot terms, checked against decks/faa-private-pilot:
- REIL (`what-is-reil`) matches `ppl…what-are-reil` (AIM 2-1-3 and PHAK use the same wording). No contradiction.
- Stabilised approach (`what-is-stabilised-approach`) uses the IPH definition: constant speed, descent rate, vertical flight path and configuration. The private-pilot card uses the AFH definition: a constant-angle glide path to a point on the runway. The two are different views of the same thing, and they don't conflict. The IPH limit of 1,000 fpm below 1,000 ft AGL also fits the AFH go-around gate of 1,000 ft in IMC.
- RVR (`what-is-rvr`) has no private-pilot card to conflict with. Its explanation is only partly right (see below).
