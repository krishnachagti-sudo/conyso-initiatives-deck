## Naming

NASAA's own name for the exam is "Uniform Investment Adviser Law Examination,
commonly called the 'Series 65' examination" (study guide p3). FINRA's page
title is "Series 65 – Uniform Investment Adviser Law Exam". NASAA's trademark
or naming guidance could not be read (nasaa.org is blocked to automated
access), and no trademark claim on "Series 65" appears in either NASAA PDF or
on the FINRA page. The study guide asserts copyright over the questions only:
"All examination questions are copyright material of NASAA. NASAA expressly
prohibits the unauthorized use of the examination and examination questions."
(p4). Cards must never reproduce or reconstruct exam questions (CONTENT-POLICY
§2).

CONTENT-POLICY §4 applies in its general form: plain text, our brand first,
and the "Everyone else" notice. Because the trademark owner is not verified,
the notice below names NASAA as the exam's developer rather than claiming a
trademark. Title pattern: "[Site name] deck for the Uniform Investment Adviser
Law Examination (Series 65)". Never "Series 65 Flashcards" or "Official".

> **Deck notice:** The Uniform Investment Adviser Law Examination (Series 65)
> is developed by the North American Securities Administrators Association,
> Inc. (NASAA) and administered by FINRA. This deck is independent and is not
> affiliated with, sponsored, endorsed or approved by NASAA or FINRA.

If NASAA's site terms, read by hand, show "Series 65" as a NASAA mark, switch
to the policy's wording: "Series 65 is a trademark of NASAA. This deck is
independent and is not affiliated with, sponsored, endorsed or approved by
NASAA."

## Outline

The owner's order and weights (outline, effective 12 June 2023). Leaf items
are grouped; every numbered item maps to at least one concept. The study guide
(pp12–14) also names the statutes and rules eligible for testing; they are
listed under IV.

**I. Economic Factors and Business Information: 15%, 20 questions**
- A. Basic Economic Concepts: business cycles; monetary and fiscal policies; global factors (currency valuation and effective exchange rates, sovereign debt, geopolitical); inflation/deflation; interest rates, yield curves, credit spreads; economic indicators (GDP, employment indicators, trade deficit, CPI) `[T1]`
- B. Financial Reporting: income statement, balance sheet, statement of cash flow, auditor disclosures (qualified vs unqualified), corporate SEC filings, annual reports; audited v. unaudited; cash v. accrual accounting `[T2]`
- C. Analytical Methods: time value of money (IRR, NPV, FV); descriptive statistics (mean, median, mode, range, standard deviation, Alpha, Beta, Sharpe, correlation); current, quick and debt-to-equity ratios; price-to-earnings and price-to-book `[T3]`
- D. Types of Risk: systematic (interest rate, sector, geopolitical); unsystematic (credit, legal/regulatory, financial, issuer-specific); opportunity cost; capital structure and liquidation priority `[T3]`

**II. Investment Vehicle Characteristics: 25%, 32 questions**
- A. Cash and cash equivalents: insured deposits (demand deposits, CDs); money market instruments (commercial paper, Treasury bills) `[T4]`
- B. Fixed income types: Treasury securities, TIPS; asset-backed; corporate; municipal (GO, revenue, insured, tax implications); foreign sovereign and corporate `[T4]`
- C. Fixed income characteristics and valuation: tax implications, ratings, liquidity, liquidation preference, call features, coupon v. zero coupon, duration, pricing (par, premium, discount), risk, yield; maturity, YTC, YTM, coupon, conversion valuation, credit spread, discounted cash flow `[T4]`
- D. Equity types: common (domestic, foreign, ADR); preferred, convertible preferred, floating rate preferred `[T5]`
- E. Equity characteristics: voting rights, preemptive right, liquidation preferences; restricted stock and resale restrictions; dividends; incentive and nonqualified employee stock options `[T5]`
- F. Equity valuation: technical analysis, fundamental analysis, dividend discount, discounted cash flow `[T5]`
- G. Equity public offering: IPO, secondary offering, SPACs/blind pools/blank check `[T5]`
- H. Pooled investment types: open-end and closed-end mutual funds; private funds (hedge, private equity, venture capital); UITs; ETFs; REITs (liquid and non-liquid) `[T6]`
- I. Pooled investment characteristics: share classes, liquidity, tax, fees, NAV and premium/discount, benefits and risks, comparisons (benchmarks, manager tenure, change in policy, style) `[T6]`
- J–K. Derivatives: options and warrants, futures (definitions); costs, benefits and risks `[T7]`
- L. Alternatives: limited partnerships, ETNs, leveraged funds, inverse funds, structured products `[T7]`
- M. Insurance-based products: variable, fixed and indexed annuities; whole, term, universal and variable life `[T7]`
- N. Other assets: commodities and precious metals; digital assets (distinction, characteristics, risks; securities, currencies and assets) `[T7]`

**III. Client Investment Recommendations and Strategies: 30%, 39 questions**
- A. Type of client: individuals and sole proprietorships; general and limited partnerships, LLCs, C and S corporations; trusts and estates; foundations and charities `[T8]`
- B. Client profile: goals; financial situation (cash flow, balance sheet, investments, tax, Social Security and pensions); risk tolerance; nonfinancial considerations (ESG and religious values, experience, life events and stage, behavioural finance); data gathering; time horizon `[T8]`
- C. Capital market theory: CAPM, Modern Portfolio Theory, Efficient Market Hypothesis `[T9]`
- D. Portfolio management: strategic and tactical allocation; active, passive, growth, value, income, capital appreciation; diversification, sector rotation, dollar-cost averaging, options, leverage, volatility management, inverse strategies, high frequency trading `[T9]`
- E. Tax: capital gains, qualified dividends, basis, marginal bracket, AMT, retirement distributions and RMDs, government benefit implications (IRMAA); corporate (C vs S), trusts, pass-throughs (REIT, MLP, LLC); estate and gift tax (exemptions, unified credit, annual vs lifetime, portability) `[T10]`
- F. Retirement plans: traditional and Roth IRAs; Solo 401(k); qualified plans (DB, DC, 401(k), 403(b), 457, SIMPLE IRA, SEP); nonqualified plans `[T11]`
- G. ERISA: fiduciary issues (investment choices, QDIA, diversification; study guide adds ERISA § 404(c)); investment policy statement; prohibited transactions `[T11]`
- H. Special accounts: 529s, Coverdell; UTMA/UGMA; HSAs `[T11]`
- I. Ownership and estate planning: JTWROS, TIC, TBE, CPWROS; TOD/POD; beneficiary designation and per stirpes; trusts and wills; QDROs; donor advised funds `[T12]`
- J. Trading: bids, offers, market/limit/stop orders, short sale, cash and margin accounts, principal or agency trades, payment for order flow; roles of introducing and clearing broker-dealers, custodians, market makers and exchanges; commissions, markups, spread; best execution `[T12]`
- K. Performance measures: risk-adjusted, time-weighted, dollar-weighted, annualised, total, holding period, IRR, expected, inflation-adjusted and after-tax returns; current yield; benchmarks `[T9]`

**IV. Laws, Regulations, and Guidelines Including Prohibition on Unethical Business Practices: 30%, 39 questions**
- A. Investment advisers, state-registered and federal covered: definitions (incl. exempt reporting adviser); notice filing; registration, books and records, maintenance, IAR CE; IAR supervision. ## Traps

- `[T13]` The tested state law is the **Uniform Securities Act of 1956 as amended by NASAA**. Courses and websites that quote the 2002 Act's section numbers are off-syllabus; the study guide says the Series 65 "does not test" the 2002 Act.
- `[T15]` One exception to the above: the study guide lists the NASAA "Contents of Investment Advisory Contract Model Rule USA 2002 502(c)". Keep that model rule's citation as the study guide gives it.
- `[T13]` Question wording may not name the rule: "a question that begins, 'Under the Uniform Securities Act . . .' … might be testing a NASAA model rule promulgated thereunder" (study guide p7).
- `[all]` Outdated outlines are still hosted by NASAA (2011 and 2016 test specifications). Use only the June 2023 outline.
- `[all]` Counts: 140 questions, 130 scored; pass is 92 **of 130**, not of 140.
- `[T13]` SEC vs state registration: IAA 203A generally bars advisers with assets under management below $100,000,000 from SEC registration (15 U.S.C. 80b-3a), and Rule 203A-1 adds a buffer: an adviser "may, but [is] not required to register" with the SEC between $100,000,000 and $110,000,000, and "need not withdraw" unless under $90,000,000 (part 275 p13). Do not merge the figures.
- `[T13]` National de minimis: a state may not require registration of an adviser with no place of business there and "fewer than 6 clients who are residents of that State" (80b-18a). Fewer than six, not six.
- `[T13]` Private fund adviser exemption: advisers solely to private funds with US assets under management "less than $150,000,000" (IAA 203, 80b-3; Rule 203(m)-1, part 275 p12). Exempt reporting advisers report under Rule 204-4 (p25).
- `[T15]` Qualified client thresholds: Rule 205-3 sets them by an inflation formula ("multiplying $1,500,000 times the quotient…"); the current dollar amounts are set by SEC order and were not verified. Never write amounts from memory.
- `[T15]` Pay to play (Rule 206(4)-5): two-year time-out after a contribution; de minimis $350 per election where the covered associate may vote, $150 otherwise (part 275 p46). MSRB G-37 is tier D: cite the rule number only.
- `[T15]` IAA 205: no assignment of an advisory contract "without the consent of the other party to the contract".
- `[T5]` The outline says "nonqualified" stock options; IRS Pub 525 and the Series 7 registry say "nonstatutory stock option". Use the registry term; mention the outline's word in the card.
- `[T11]` The outline's "Solo 401(k)" is the IRS's "one-participant 401(k) plan". ERISA § 404(c) is 29 U.S.C. 1104(c) ("Control over assets by participant or beneficiary").
- `[T10]` Gift annual exclusion is $19,000 for both 2025 and 2026 (IRS gift tax FAQ table). Estate tax filing threshold for 2026 deaths is $15,000,000 ($13,990,000 for 2025) (IRS estate tax page). Both `volatile: true`.
- `[T12]` Settlement is T+1 (SIE brief): the investor.gov page "Settling Securities Transactions, T+2" is stale. Do not cite it.
- `[T4]` `[T5]` OpenStax is 2e ("principles-finance-2e"); the first edition is also online with different section numbers. Cite 2e only.
- `[T1]` The outline's economic indicators are GDP, employment, trade deficit and CPI. Leading and lagging indicators are not on this outline (they are in the SIE deck).
