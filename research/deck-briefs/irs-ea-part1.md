## Naming

- The IRS site calls the exam the "Special Enrollment Examination (SEE)" and
  the "IRS Enrolled Agent Special Enrollment Exam (EA-SEE)" `[become-ea.txt]`.
  PSI names the part "SEE1: Part 1 — Individuals". I found no trademark claim
  for "SEE" or "Enrolled Agent" on the pages fetched.
- IRS content policy (https://www.irs.gov/about-irs/use-of-content-from-irsgov):
  "Content on this website that was created or maintained by federal employees
  in the course of their duties is not subject to copyright and may be freely
  copied. Credit is requested." It also says: "Federal law prohibits use of any
  symbol, emblem, seal, insignia or badge of any entity of the Department of
  Treasury (including the Internal Revenue Service) or any colorable imitation
  of such words, initials, symbols, emblems, or seals in connection with any
  advertisement, solicitation, business activity, or product where such use
  could reasonably be interpreted as conveying the false impression that such
  advertisement, solicitation, business activity, or product is in any manner
  approved, endorsed, sponsored, or authorized by … the Department of the
  Treasury…" (citing 18 U.S.C. § 701, 31 U.S.C. § 333, 31 C.F.R. Part 37).
- **How CONTENT-POLICY.md §4 applies.** The IRS is not in §5, so the deck can
  name the exam, in plain text and with our brand first. The restriction
  above is stronger than an ordinary trademark rule: no IRS seal, logo or
  imitation, and nothing that suggests endorsement. Credit the IRS as the source
  (it asks for credit). Title, for example: "[Site name] deck for the IRS
  Special Enrollment Examination (SEE), Part 1: Individuals". Never "Official",
  never "IRS deck". Do not use PSI's name or logo beyond the fact that it runs
  the exam.
- **Deck notice** (the §4 "Everyone else" pattern, adapted because no trademark
  was found): "The Special Enrollment Examination (SEE) is an examination of
  the Internal Revenue Service, administered by PSI Services. This deck is
  independent and is not affiliated with, sponsored, endorsed or approved by
  the Internal Revenue Service, the U.S. Department of the Treasury or PSI
  Services. It uses IRS publications, which are works of the U.S. Government.
  Tax amounts are for tax year 2025."

## Outline (Part 1, bulletin pp. 29–33, owner's order)

Weights are scored questions out of 85.

**1. Preliminary Work and Taxpayer Data — 14 (16%)**
- Preliminary work to prepare tax returns [T1]
- Use of prior years' returns for comparison, accuracy, and carryovers [T1]
- Taxpayer personal information (date of birth, marital status, dependents, IP PIN, photo ID) [T1]
- Residency status and/or citizenship (visas, green cards, resident or non-resident alien, ITIN) [T1]
- Filing requirements and due date [T1]
- Taxpayer filing status [T1]
- Sources of worldwide taxable and non-taxable income [T1]
- Sources of applicable exclusions and adjustments to gross income [T1][T3]
- Sources of applicable deductions (itemized, standard) [T1][T4]
- Qualifications for dependency [T1]
- Sources of applicable credits [T1][T4]
- Sources of tax payments and refundable credits [T1][T4]
- Previous IRS correspondence with taxpayer [T1]
- Additional required returns to be filed, and taxes paid (employment, gifts, international information and other information returns) [T1]
- Special filing requirements (foreign income, presidentially declared disaster areas, injured spouse) [T1]
- Foreign account and asset reporting (FBAR, Form 8938) [T1][T7]
- Minor children's unearned income ("Kiddie" tax) [T1]
- ACA requirements (household income, premium tax credit, household size) [T1][T4]

**2. Income and Assets — 17 (20%)**
- *Income:* taxability of wages, salaries and other earnings; interest; dividends and other distributions; personal property rental; gambling income and deductions; cancellation of debt; U.S. citizen or resident with foreign income (treaties, Forms 1116, 2555, 3520, 5471); other income (scholarships, barter, hobby, alimony, combat pay, unearned income, taxable recoveries, NOL, illegal income); constructive receipt; constructive dividends; passive income and loss; pass-through income (K-1, basis, QBI items); royalties; state and local refunds and other recoveries; 1099-MISC, 1099-NEC, 1099-K reporting and corrections [T2]
- *Retirement income:* basis in a traditional IRA (Form 8606); traditional and Roth IRAs; distributions from qualified and non-qualified plans (rollovers, 1099-R, QCD); excess contributions; early distribution penalties and exceptions; prohibited transactions; conversions and recharacterization; required minimum distributions; loans from qualified plans; Social Security and Railroad Retirement benefits; inherited retirement accounts; foreign pensions [T2]
- *Property, real and personal:* sales and depreciation recapture, 1099-A; capital gains and losses (netting, mark-to-market, virtual currency); basis (purchased, gifted, inherited); basis after stock splits and stock dividends; publicly traded partnerships; sale of a personal residence (section 121); installment sales; options (stock, commodity, ISO, ESPP); like-kind exchange; non-business bad debts; investor versus trader [T3]
- *Adjustments to income:* self-employment tax; retirement contribution limits and deductibility; health savings accounts; other adjustments (student loan interest, alimony, military moving expenses, write-in adjustments); self-employed health insurance [T3]

**3. Deductions and Credits — 17 (20%)**
- *Itemized deductions and QBI:* medical, dental, vision, long-term care; taxes (state and local, personal property, real estate); interest (mortgage, investment, tracing, points, indebtedness limits); charitable contributions; nonbusiness casualty and theft losses; other itemized deductions; itemized deductions for Form 1040-NR; QBI deduction [T4]
- *Credits:* child and dependent care; child tax credit and credit for other dependents; education credits; foreign tax credit; earned income tax credit (preparer checklist, eligibility, disallowance); adoption credits; ACA premium tax credit; other credits (health coverage tax credit, energy credits, retirement savings contribution credit) [T4]

**4. Taxation — 15 (18%)**
- AMT and credit for prior-year minimum tax; household employees; underpayment penalties and interest; self-employment tax; excess Social Security withholding; clergy; military; income in respect of a decedent; net investment income tax; Additional Medicare Tax; uncollected Social Security and Medicare tax; other taxes (first-time homebuyer credit repayment) [T5]

**5. Advising the Individual Taxpayer — 11 (13%)**
- Reporting obligations (1099, bartering, cash); property sales (homes, stock, businesses, antiques, collectibles); education planning (lifetime learning credit, section 529 plans); estate planning (gift versus inheritance, trusts, family partnerships, charitable giving, long-term care, life insurance); retirement planning (annuities, IRAs, employer plans, early retirement, RMDs, beneficiaries, charitable distributions from an IRA); marriage and divorce (settlements, common-law, community property, alimony); items that affect future or past returns (carryovers, NOL, Schedule D, Form 8801, negative QBI carryover); injured spouse; innocent spouse; estimated tax and penalty avoidance; adjustments, deductions and credits for planning (timing); character of transaction (capital versus ordinary rates); MFJ, MFS and HOH compared (joint and several liability); conditions for a claim for refund (amended returns); penalty of perjury [T6]

**6. Specialized Returns for Individuals — 11 (13%)**
- *Estate tax:* gross estate, taxable estate, unified credit; jointly held property; marital deduction and portability; life insurance, IRAs and retirement plans; filing requirements and due dates (Forms 706 and 1041) [T7]
- *Gift tax:* gift-splitting; annual exclusion; unified credit; effect on estate tax (GST tax); filing requirements (Form 709) [T7]
- *International information reporting:* filing requirements and due dates (FBAR, Forms 8938, 8865, 5471, 3520); covered accounts; potential penalties (failure to file, underreporting, statute of limitations); distinctions between FBAR and Form 8938 [T7]

## Traps

- [T1] The vendor changed. Prometric ran the SEE until 1 March 2026; PSI runs it now. Older guides, links and the Prometric bulletin are out of date.
- [T1] This window opened on 1 July 2026, not 1 May: the FAQ still says the test is "generally" offered from 1 May, but the 2026-27 window is 1 July 2026 to 28 February 2027.
- [T1] The window tests tax year 2025, not 2026. The 2026 editions now on irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf (Pub 505, Pub 926, Form 706 instructions) give 2026 amounts. Pub 590-A already lists the 2026 IRA limit ($7,500) next to the 2025 limit ($7,000) (p590a p1–2).
- [T4] 2025 is the first year of the 2025 legislation's new amounts: standard deduction $15,750 / $31,500 / $23,625; SALT limit $40,000 ($20,000 MFS); CTC $2,200. Earlier figures in study material are wrong for this window (p17 p3; i1040s8 p1).
- [T4] The four new Schedule 1-A deductions (tips, overtime, car loan interest, enhanced senior deduction) are taken whether the taxpayer itemizes or not (p17 p3).
- [T4] Tips and overtime deductions need a valid SSN and, if married, a joint return; the senior deduction too (p17 p3).
- [T4] Credit for other dependents: from 2025 the taxpayer (or spouse if joint) needs a valid SSN or ITIN issued by the return's due date (p17 p4).
- [T4] Adoption credit: from 2025, up to $5,000 per child is refundable (p17 p4).
- [T4] Residential clean energy credits cannot be claimed for expenditures after 31 December 2025 (i5695 p1): 2025 is still in, 2026 is not.
- [T4] For tax year 2025, taxpayers above 400% of the federal poverty line may still be allowed a PTC (p974 p1).
- [T2] The qualified principal residence indebtedness exclusion still applies in 2025; Pub 4681 says it "cannot be excluded" for discharges after 2025 (p4681 p1).
- [T4] Pub 972 (child tax credit) is no longer current: its last edition is for 2020. Use the 2025 Schedule 8812 instructions.
- [T4] AOTC versus LLC: $2,500 per eligible student versus $2,000 per return; 40% of the AOTC may be refundable (p970 p10, p22).
- [T1] Qualifying child versus qualifying relative: different tests; tie-breaker rules apply when a child qualifies for more than one person (p501).
- [T1] Kiddie tax: Pub 929 is no longer on irs.gov (404); use Form 8615 (2025) and Pub 17, which refers to Form 8615 (p17 p57).
- [T2] Alimony: taxable to the recipient only under instruments executed before 2019 (p504).
- [T2] RMDs start at age 73 ("Age 73 for tax years 2023 and later", p590b p6). Older material gives other ages.
- [T2] Inherited IRAs are subject to the 10-year rule in Pub 590-B (p590b p2, p9).
- [T3] Basis of gifted versus inherited property follows different rules (p551).
- [T4] Mortgage interest: $750,000 limit ($375,000 MFS) for debt taken out after 15 December 2017 (p936 p1).
- [T4] Personal casualty losses are limited to federally declared disasters for tax years after 2017 (p547 p2).
- [T5] NIIT (3.8%) and Additional Medicare Tax (0.9%) are two different taxes with separate forms (8960, 8959).
- [T6] Injured spouse (Form 8379) is not innocent spouse relief (Pub 971). Candidates confuse the two.
- [T7] FBAR versus Form 8938: FBAR is filed with FinCEN when aggregate foreign accounts exceed $10,000 at any time; Form 8938 is filed with the return and has higher thresholds ($50,000 / $75,000 for unmarried taxpayers in the U.S.) (irs.gov comparison page).
- [T7] The estate and gift basic exclusion amount for 2025 is $13,990,000; the gift annual exclusion is $19,000 (i709 p1; i706 Rev. Sept. 2025 p1).
