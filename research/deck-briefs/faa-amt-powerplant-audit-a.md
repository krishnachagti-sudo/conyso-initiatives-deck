# faa-amt-powerplant audit, part a: topic 1, Reciprocating engines

318 cards checked (the 317 written, plus the overhaul primer added below). Sources: FAA-H-8083-32B (cached text, grepped by page), AC 43.13-1B, 14 CFR parts 43 and 65 (cached). Every number in theory, construction, defects, overhaul and storage was compared with the handbook page. All 62 scenario cards had their choicesExplained letters checked against the choices and key, and none was mislettered. The three calculation scenarios (IHP, BHP, displacement) were worked again and are right.

## Checker fixes made directly in `decks/faa-amt-powerplant/notes/01-reciprocating-engines.json`
- 29 fronts that did not ask a question: 23 `defect-*` fronts now read "Which engine-part defect term describes …?". Six "Name two …" fronts now read "What are two …?": opposed-advantages, carbon-effects, hydraulic-lifter-benefits, overlap-purposes, ve-reducers and barrel-measurements.
- 17 uses of "overhaul" before any primer: added the primer `amtp.reciprocating-engines.overhaul` (order 1188, before pre-oiling). Its evidence is "Each engine manufacturer sets a total time in service when the engine should be removed from service and overhauled." (p. 10-1, PDF p. 369). Concept: amtp.overhaul-procedure.
- Deck-level rule: core concept amtp.engine-preservation-rts (fact card spray-positions) had no application card. It was added to the conceptIDs of before-turning-prop, which applies the return-to-service steps on p. 8-20.

overhaul-supercharger | wrong | Keyed "No: a powerplant mechanic may not perform or supervise the major overhaul of an internally supercharged engine". This misreads a garbled handbook sentence. 14 CFR 65.81(a) lets a mechanic perform or supervise the maintenance he is rated for, excluding only major repairs to propellers. The real limit is on approval for return to service. Rewritten on 65.87(a) and 65.95(a)(1). | 65.81(a): "A certificated mechanic may perform or supervise the maintenance, preventive maintenance or alteration of an aircraft or appliance, or a part thereof, for which he is rated (but excluding major repairs to, and major alterations of, propellers…)" (Part 65 PDF p. 13); 65.87(a): "may approve and return to service a powerplant … (excluding major repairs and major alterations)" (PDF p. 14)
mechanic-major-overhaul | wrong | Same misreading, presented as a condition on performing or supervising the work. Checked and found right, among others: V-type banks 60° apart; nose section of 1,000–2,500 hp engines; 18-cylinder rods 2 and 16; firing-order numbers 9/5 and 11/7; pistons matched to within one-fourth ounce; ignition 20°–35° before TDC; intake valve closes 50°–75° after BDC; friction 10–15 percent of IHP; heat split 25–30/15–20/5–10/40–45 percent; mechanical efficiency near 90 percent; propeller 80 percent; flame speed 100 ft/s; warm-up at 1,000–1,600 rpm; oil pressure within 30 s and 1 min; valve-seat runout 0.002 in; oversizes 0.010/0.015/0.020/0.030 in; interference fit 0.5° or 1.5°; storage periods of 30 and 90 days; silica gel at 30 and 60 percent; containers pressurised to 5 psi; humidity indicators checked every 30, 90 and 180 days; Rotax oil mix 1:50. Every distractor claim was grepped: head wind and rpm (p. 10-28), splined shafts on high-output radials (p. 1-23), porous Oilite bushings (p. 1-22), flyweights "not a true part" (p. 1-8).
