## Naming

- **Trademark statement.** Syllabus page 2 has "ISTQB® is a registered
  trademark of the International Software Testing Qualifications Board."
  `[syllabus.txt p2]`. The same sentence appears in the Exam Structures and
  Rules and the tables. ISTQB's site uses "ISTQB®" throughout. I found no
  statement that "CTFL" or "Certified Tester Foundation Level" is a registered
  mark, and no trademark or logo policy page. The sitemap has no such page,
  and `/privacy-notice-and-terms-of-use/` covers only privacy.
- **CONTENT-POLICY §4 and §5.** ISTQB is not listed in §5, so the deck may
  name the exam, with the default notice from the "Everyone else" row, and
  may map cards to the LOs (`ExamRefs`). Title pattern: `[Site name] deck for
  the ISTQB® Certified Tester Foundation Level (CTFL) exam`. Plain text, our
  brand first, no ISTQB logo, and never "official" or "accredited". ISTQB uses
  "accredited" for Member Board-assessed training `[ctfl-page.txt]`, so the
  deck must not suggest it is accredited training.
- **Source tiers (CONTENT-POLICY §3).**
  - The syllabus is **tier C**. Its notice is "All rights reserved". It
    allows only non-commercial extracts with acknowledgement, use "as the
    basis for articles and books" with acknowledgement, and "Any other use of
    this syllabus is prohibited without first obtaining the approval in
    writing of the ISTQB®." `[syllabus.txt p2]`. That is not an open licence,
    and our CC BY-SA output would allow commercial reuse of any copied
    extract. So we take facts in our own words, cite the page and copy no
    wording. The Exam Structures and Rules and the tables carry the same
    notice (tier C).
  - The **ISTQB Glossary is tier B.** The glossary site's footer string reads
    "Except where otherwise noted, content on this site is licensed under a
    Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International license"
    `[gloss-en.json]`. Definitions can be quoted with attribution, and every
    keyword primer should use its glossary entry. All 97 syllabus keywords
    resolved on the glossary API, saved in `[glossary/*.json]`.
  - The lead said that the syllabus is published free with a notice
    "permitting some use". That is true, but the use it permits is narrow,
    and the free open source here is the glossary, not the syllabus.
  - **For the lawyer (§8):** does a free flashcard deck count as an "article"
    or "book" based on the syllabus? The deck relies on paraphrase (tier C),
    so it does not depend on the answer.

## Traps

- `[T1]` **Which level are keywords tested at?** Syllabus 0.5 says keywords
  "shall be remembered (K1)" `[p11]`. Exam Rules v1.2 (2 May 2025) changed
  rule 4.1.4 to "Examinable for understanding their definition (K2)". Primers
  should teach meaning, not just recall.
- `[T1]` Testing and QA are not synonyms. Testing is "product-oriented,
  corrective" and QA is "process-oriented, preventive" `[p17]`. v4.0.1
  replaced "QC" with "testing" in 1.2.2 `[p73]`, and older material compares
  QA with QC.
- `[T1]` Keep the chain straight: root cause, error (mistake), defect (fault,
  bug), failure. Failures can also come from environmental conditions, and
  some defects never cause a failure `[p17]`.
- `[T1]` Debugging is not testing. After static testing, debugging needs no
  reproduction or diagnosis `[p16]`.
- `[T1]` The absence-of-defects fallacy (principle 7) is about validation,
  not about testing being unable to prove correctness (principle 1).
- `[T2]` There are two roles, the test management role and the testing role,
  and one person may hold both `[p21]`.
- `[T2]` Independence of testing moved from Chapter 5 to Chapter 1 in v4.0
  `[p75]`. Its four levels are
  author, peers, outside the team and outside the organisation `[p22]`.
- `[T3]` v4.0 split integration testing into two test levels, component
  integration testing and system integration testing `[p75]`, so there are
  five levels. v3.1 material shows four.
- `[T3]` ISO/IEC 25010 (2023) renamed usability to "interaction capability"
  and portability to "flexibility", and added "safety". The syllabus keeps the
  old names and gives the new ones in brackets `[p30, p72]`.
- `[T3]` Shift left does not mean neglecting later testing `[p27]`.
- `[T3]` Confirmation testing checks the fix. Regression testing checks for
  adverse effects elsewhere `[p30]`.
- `[T4]` Who leads which review: a walkthrough is led by the author, a
  technical review by a moderator. In an inspection the author "cannot act as
  the review leader or scribe" `[p36–37]`. Moderator = facilitator and
  scribe = recorder. Manager and review leader are different roles `[p36]`.
- `[T4]` v4.0 removed review techniques as a K3 LO `[p75]`.
- `[T5]` 2-value BVA has two coverage items per boundary. 3-value BVA has
  three, some of which are not boundary values `[p40]`.
- `[T5]` State transition coverage runs weakest to strongest: all states,
  then valid transitions (0-switch), then all transitions. The last "should
  be a minimum requirement for mission and safety-critical software"
  `[p42]`. The model is now called a "state diagram", not a "state transition
  diagram" `[p73]`.
- `[T5]` Syllabus errata. Appendix C says 4.2.1 gained "invalid equivalence
  partitions should be tested in isolation to avoid defect masking"
  `[p73]`. But section 4.2.1 of v4.0.1 has no such sentence. "Masking"
  appears only in 4.2.4 (all transitions coverage, p42) and the index. Cite
  4.2.4 for defect masking.
- `[T5]` EP's 100% coverage includes invalid partitions `[p40]`. Each Choice
  coverage ignores combinations `[p40]`.
- `[T6]` Decision testing and coverage were replaced by branch testing and
  coverage in v4.0. Use case testing was removed `[p75]`. Branch coverage
  subsumes statement coverage, but not the other way round `[p43]`. The old
  claim that "100% decision coverage implies 100% statement coverage" was
  withdrawn as flawed `[p75]`.
- `[T6]` The ATDD order is positive tests, then negative, then non-functional
  `[p46]`. ATDD is covered in both 2.1.3 (K1) and 4.5.3 (K3).
- `[T7]` Test pyramid: the higher the layer, the *lower* the isolation.
  v4.0.1 corrected this `[p51, p73]`.
- `[T7]` Quadrants: usability testing is in Q3. Q4 holds non-functional tests
  "except usability tests" `[p51]`.
- `[T7]` Three-point estimation: E = (a + 4m + b) / 6 and SD = (b − a) / 6.
  The worked example is 10 ± 2 person-hours `[p50]`. The ratio example: 3:2
  development-to-test with 600 person-days of development gives 400
  person-days of testing `[p49]`.
- `[T7]` In v4.0.1, "completion criteria" means binary yes/no criteria, not a
  synonym for exit criteria `[p73]`. Definition of Done = exit criteria and
  Definition of Ready = entry criteria in Agile `[p49]`.
- `[T8]` Scope creep and poor tool support are *project* risks (technical
  issues), not product risks `[p52]`. Risk likelihood is "greater than zero
  and less than one" `[p52]`.
- `[T8]` Severity (degree of impact) and priority (to fix) are separate
  fields of a defect report `[p57]`. ISO/IEC/IEEE 29119-3 calls defect
  reports "incident reports" and test progress reports "test status reports"
  `[p55, p57]`.
- `[T9]` Tool selection, pilot projects and introducing tools were removed in
  v4.0 `[p75]`.
- **Outdated material in circulation.** v3.1 content was retired from
  exams after the sunset dates above, but anything that mentions use case
  testing, decision coverage, review techniques as a K3 LO, a test
  strategies section, or "integration testing" as a single level is pre-v4.0
  `[p75]`. The exam has no K4 questions ("K4 0" in the CTFL table).
- **Glossary tagging gap.** Eight syllabus keywords are not tagged as
  Foundation v4.0 keywords in the glossary API, though they resolve there:
  test process, test result, black-box testing, functional testing,
  white-box testing, boundary value analysis, statement coverage and test
  plan `[glossary/*.json]`. The syllabus keyword list governs.
- **Spelling.** ISTQB writes US spelling ("behavior", "prioritization",
  "analyze"). Terms keep the source spelling (CARD-STANDARD open question).
  Our prose is British.

## Languages

The exam is taken in the Member Board's or Exam Provider's language. A
candidate whose native language differs from the exam language gets 25% more
time, 75 minutes in all `[exam-rules.txt; ctfl-page.txt]`. Member Boards
translate the syllabus: "Any ISTQB®-recognized Member Board may translate
this syllabus provided they reproduce the abovementioned Copyright Notice"
`[p2]`. The v4.0 release notes gave non-English Member Boards 18 months to
move to v4.0 `[release-notes.txt]`. These translations are tier C like the
English original. The ISTQB Glossary (CC BY 4.0) holds translations in 24
locales: cs_CZ, da_DK, de_DE, en_US, es_ES, fr_FR, he_IL, hu_HU, in_ID, it_IT,
ja_JP, ka_GE, ko_KR, nl_NL, no_NO, pl_PL, pt_BR, pt_PT, ru_RU, sk_SK, sv_SE,
tr_TR, uk_UA and zh_CN. This list was read from the language list of the
"test basis" entry `[glossary/test-basis.json]`, so it is an openly licensed
base for translated primers.

## Not verified

- **Retake rules and fees.** ISTQB's help pages say exams and certificates
  are handled by Member Boards and Exam Providers. I found no retake waiting
  period, attempt limit or price on istqb.org (CTFL page, help pages
