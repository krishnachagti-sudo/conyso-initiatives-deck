# faa-private-pilot audit D: topics 12–16

Scope: decks/faa-private-pilot/notes/12-airport-operations-and-communications.json to 16-seaplane-and-multiengine.json, 366 cards (142 + 79 + 84 + 32 + 29). I checked every card against its `evidence` first. Claims that go beyond the evidence were checked in research/sources/faa-private-pilot/: AIM 2-3, 4-1, 4-2, 4-3, 4-4, 6-2 and 6-3; PHAK ch. 14; AFH ch. 5, 6, 7, 9, 10, 11, 13 and 18; 14 CFR parts 61 and 91 and 49 CFR part 830 (eCFR); and the Private Pilot Airplane ACS. Nothing was fetched.

**Seaplane check.** The seaplane cards in topic 16 (water-keep-clear, water-crossing, water-overtaking, app-water-head-on, acs-porpoising-skipping, acs-marine-zones, acs-water-surface, acs-post-landing) claim nothing beyond 14 CFR § 91.115 and the ACS task elements they quote. **Airport signs, light-gun signals and radio.** The light-gun cards match § 91.125 and the AIM 4-3-13 table. The sign, marking and radio cards match AIM 2-3, 4-1, 4-2, 4-3 and 4-4, apart from the findings below.

**Emergency and night cards.** These match AFH ch. 9, 11 and 18, PHAK ch. 14, AIM 6-2 and 6-3, and 49 CFR part 830, apart from the ELT test-window overstatement and the two primers listed below.

## Findings

id | severity | problem | evidence (source words, with page)
---|---|---|---
ppl.airport-operations-and-communications.exit-runway-clear | wrong | The back says you are clear when all parts are past the holding position markings. The AIM's definition says past the runway *edge*, with nothing restricting further movement beyond the markings. The evidence quoted is a different sentence. | AIM 4-3-21: "An aircraft is considered clear of the runway when all parts of the aircraft are past the runway edge and there are no restrictions to its continued movement beyond the runway holding position markings."
ppl.airport-operations-and-communications.tower-to-ground-after-landing | unsupported | The explanation says "The tower resolves conflicts with other ground traffic before handing you to ground control". The source does not say this. | AIM 4-3-14 says only: "should not change from the tower frequency to the ground control frequency until directed to do so by the controller."
ppl.airport-operations-and-communications.relocated-threshold-use | unsupported | "check the NOTAM for the closed length" is not in the cited page. The page says relocation also shortens the opposite-direction runway. | PHAK p. 14-5: "it closes not only a set portion of the approach end of a runway, but also shortens the length of the opposite direction runway."
ppl.airport-operations-and-communications.sign-location | unsupported | The explanation says "Location signs carry no arrows". AIM 2-3-9 does not say this. | AIM 2-3-9: "The inscription is the designation of the taxiway on which the aircraft is located."
ppl.airport-operations-and-communications.hold-short-readback-required | minor | The explanation credits "The FAA handbook", but the card cites AIM 4-3-18. The "verbatim" wording is from PHAK p. 14-32. | AIM 4-3-18: "Controllers are required to request a readback of runway hold short assignment when it is not received from the pilot/vehicle."
ppl.airport-operations-and-communications.what-is-a-tetrahedron | minor | The explanation says "The handbook", but the source is the AIM. It also leaves out the AIM's warning against reading wind direction from the tetrahedron. | AIM 4-3-4: "Pilots are cautioned against using a tetrahedron to indicate wind direction."
ppl.airport-operations-and-communications.segmented-circle-figure | unclear | "enlarging this indicator puts the base leg east of final" is hard to follow. A newcomer would read it twice. | AIM 4-3-4: indicators "used to indicate the direction of turns when there is a variation from the normal left traffic pattern"
ppl.takeoffs-landings-and-manoeuvres.app-sideslip-drift | ambiguous | Choice C, "Reduce the bank to the left", can be read as "bank further left". The aeroplane is banked right, into the wind. | AFH p. 9-16: "Varying the amount of bank allows the pilot to drift either to the left or to the right"
ppl.emergencies.elt-test-window | minor | The back says "Only during the first 5 minutes". The AIM also allows a test outside that window if it is coordinated with the nearest FAA control tower. | AIM 6-2-4: "If operational tests must be made outside of this period, they should be coordinated with the nearest FAA Control Tower."
ppl.emergencies.what-is-distress | unclear | This is a primer, but the back gives only the radio priority of distress, not what a distress condition is. The definition (Pilot/Controller Glossary) is not cached. Needs research. | AIM 6-3-1 gives priority only: "Distress communications have absolute priority over all other communications"
ppl.emergencies.what-is-urgency | unclear | Same as above for urgency. Needs research. | AIM 6-3-1: "Urgency communications have priority over all other communications except distress"
